# Port Hedland: the site against the September 2026 report

## Where this stands

**Resolved, 9 September 2026.** All 24 indicators were refreshed from the
September 2026 *Port Hedland Regional Readiness Report*. The report is now the
site's source of record for Port Hedland, on Tejesh's instruction.

- **Every score matches** and no grading scale changed. The site's grading
  scales were already the report's, so they were left untouched.
- **The five Kwinana clones are gone.** Federal skills and training,
  approvals, investment framework, R&D and procurement now carry the report's
  Port Hedland text. Kwinana mentions across the whole region fell from 63 to
  17, and all 17 that remain are the report's own comparisons between the two
  precincts, including the state indicators that explicitly benchmark against
  the Kwinana Regional Readiness Report.
- **Industry decarbonisation is complete.** The company-by-company material
  the site was missing is now in, rendered as labelled lists rather than
  tables, along with the boxed note on why fossil fuel companies are excluded.
  That section went from about 1,500 words to 3,750.
- **The Fuel Tax Credit material is in**, in federal emissions reduction.
- **References cover all 24 indicators**: 213 entries, 253 markers, every
  marker resolving to an entry.

### One deliberate departure from the report

The **Capacity Investment Scheme** passage in federal emissions reduction is
the September fact-check, not the report's wording. The report says the NWIS
"lacks the centralised wholesale spot market the CIS requires" and cites
"Allens 2024", which is not in its own reference list. The site's version also
covers the Reserve Capacity Mechanism side and the absence of central
dispatch, and is sourced to the ACCC's Pilbara ISOCo determination. It is the
one entry in `references-port-hedland.js` carrying `report: null`.

The WA approval bilateral question needed no departure: the report is right
that WA holds an assessment bilateral only, and the site now carries the
report's own text.

- **Two unresolved `(ref)` placeholders** were stripped from the published

## The five Kwinana clones — RESOLVED 9 September 2026

The report rewrote all five for Port Hedland and the eastern Pilbara, and the
site now carries that text. Recorded below is what actually changed, and three
things worth knowing about the import:

- **Two `(ref)` placeholders were stripped** from the published prose, in
  skills and training (the 11,065 additional workers by 2029 figure) and in
  procurement (the ESP Policy threshold). Both claims are now unsourced in the
  report and on the site.
- **One citation has no matching entry** in its section's reference list:
  "NZEA and RenewMap 2027" in the investment framework. The sentence is
  published without a marker.
- **Eighteen sources across the five are listed in the report but never cited
  in its own prose**, so they carry no marker: five each in skills and
  training, approvals and investment framework, two in R&D, one in
  procurement. Same pattern as the eighteen unplaced Gladstone citations.

What changed in substance, and not just region names:

- **Federal — research and development.** The projects change completely.
  Kwinana's BlueScope NeoSmelt ($19.8m), Calix ZESTY ($44.9m) and Rio Tinto's
  Yarwun hydrogen calcination ($32.1m) give way to Fortescue's Solar Innovation
  Hub ($45m), PLS/Calix Mid-Stream at Pilgangoora ($38m), Fortescue's
  low-temperature electrochemical ironmaking with Deakin and Curtin ($3m) and
  Intercontinental Energy's P2H2 digital twin ($1.56m). HILT CRC's named
  participants change from Alcoa, Fortescue, Rio Tinto and South32 to
  Fortescue, Rio Tinto, Hancock and BHP.
- **Federal — approvals.** A factual conflict, not just a region swap — now
  resolved, see *Two conflicts, checked and settled* below. The report was
  right and the site was wrong, and the wrong sentence has been corrected in
  both Port Hedland and Kwinana. The rest of the section still needs the
  report's Port Hedland text.
- **Federal — investment framework.** The worked example changes from ARENA's
  $19.8m for NeoSmelt at Kwinana to more than $128m of ARENA funding across the
  eastern Pilbara, and a new paragraph covers WA's $3 billion Rewiring the
  Nation allocation and NAIF's $90m towards APA's Chichester Solar Farm.
- **Federal — skills and training.** Rewritten around Fortescue's Power Up
  Training Centre, the transferable diesel-trades workforce and the $43.2m
  Pundulmurra TAFE upgrade, in place of Alcoa's closure and BP's Kwinana Energy
  Hub. Three new paragraphs cover the $140m Clean Energy Training and Research
  Institute, the FIFO roster problem, and the Pilbara Universities Centre.
- **Federal — procurement.** Mostly region corrections, but the production tax
  credits change from green aluminium to green hydrogen — a factual change
  worth checking against the FMiA credits — and Element Zero and AREH replace
  the Kwinana asset list.

Even in the new report, the procurement section still refers to "the Kwinana,
Pilbara, and South West supply chains" and to the WA Local Content Policy.
That may be deliberate; it reads oddly in a Port Hedland assessment.

## The other three

- **Industry decarbonisation plans.** The site carries 14 paragraphs of prose
  and none of the report's three tables — the company-by-company plans table,
  the summary scoring table, and the new-industry table — nor the boxed note
  explaining why fossil fuel companies are excluded. That is why 33 of the
  section's 51 sources could not be placed. The report also contradicts itself:
  its opening says most companies have set targets and published plans, which
  is what the site says and what a score of 2 requires, while a later paragraph
  says most Pilbara companies using the port have no clear plans. The report
  carries four unresolved `(ref)` placeholders in this section.
- **Federal — emissions reduction.** A substantial new paragraph on the Fuel
  Tax Credit scheme — $10.2 billion in 2024-25, mining's share around $4.8
  billion, BHP's $622 million claim against $17 million of Safeguard compliance
  credits, and Fortescue's proposed $50 million cap. It carries 8 of the
  section's 27 sources, none of which can be placed until the paragraph is
  added. The report also changes why the Capacity Investment Scheme does not
  reach the Pilbara: the site says the NWIS runs under a separate light-handed
  access regime, the report says it lacks the centralised wholesale spot market
  the CIS requires. Those contradict each other.
- **Effective community engagement.** The report adds a quotation from the
  Karlka Nyiyaparli Aboriginal Corporation newsletter, and reverses the
  reasoning in one paragraph: the site concludes from the data that exists,
  the report concludes from the absence of data. Both read sensibly; only one
  is what the authors meant.

## Smaller calls

- **Renewable energy generation.** The report adds a figure the site does not
  carry: 2050 green export demand "estimated around 90 GW" against 7.1 GW for
  2035. Adding it means asserting a new number in BZE's name.
- **Hydrogen network.** The site keeps a sentence on Pilbara Hydrogen Hub
  pipelines linking AREH to Burrup Peninsula demand that the report no longer
  carries. The report neither contradicts nor supports it.
- **State — skills and training.** The report drops the $16.9 million Collie
  Jobs and Skills Centre from its funding list. The site still names it.
- **State — approvals.** The report drops "entirely" from the claim that the
  Mining Act 1978 displaces development approval under the planning system.
- **Wider community benefit sharing.** The report's opening now says green
  iron where the site says green steel — but the same section still says
  "green steel superpower" further down. The report is inconsistent with
  itself.
- **Housing** and **strategic planning** differ only in where paragraphs
  break. The site runs some of the report's paragraphs together; splitting
  them would read better but changes nothing.

## Two conflicts, checked and settled

Both were checked against primary sources in September 2026 and corrected in
the evidence text. Neither changes a score.

**Does WA hold an approval bilateral with the Commonwealth? No — the report was
right.** Western Australia has an *assessment* bilateral only, in force since
1 January 2015, which accredits the EPA's assessment processes. The WA
Government's own page on the agreement says the state "is still negotiating an
approval bilateral agreement with the Australian Government". The memorandum of
understanding signed by Murray Watt and Roger Cook on 21 April 2026 commits the
two governments to a *replacement assessment* bilateral, with an approvals
agreement as a later step — industry commentary at the time read it as a
foundation for an approvals bilateral "in a couple of years time".

The site said WA held both. That sentence was live on Port Hedland **and
Kwinana**, since the Port Hedland text was cloned from it, and the claim is
about Western Australia rather than either precinct — so it was wrong in both
places. Both are now corrected.

**Why does the Capacity Investment Scheme not reach the Pilbara? Both versions
were partly right, and neither gave the operative reason.** The scheme's WA
tenders are written around the Wholesale Electricity Market: a project must
connect to the SWIS and participate in the WEM, and a CIS agreement's floor and
ceiling are settled against WEM energy revenue plus Reserve Capacity Mechanism
credits. The NWIS has neither. The ACCC's December 2024 determination on
Pilbara ISOCo puts it plainly: "A wholesale electricity spot market does not
exist in the NWIS. Instead, the market is bilaterally contracted." There is no
central dispatch and no capacity market, so there is no market revenue for a
CIS agreement to underwrite.

The site's "separate, light-handed access regime" is a true statement about the
Pilbara Networks Access Code, but that governs *network access*, not revenue —
it was the wrong cause. The report's "lacks the centralised wholesale spot
market the CIS requires" is right as far as it goes but leaves out the capacity
side, which matters just as much in the WEM. The evidence text now gives both
halves, and the ACCC determination has been added to the reference list for
that indicator. It is the one entry in `references-port-hedland.js` carrying
`report: null`, because it is not a source the report cited.

## Problems in the report itself

Recorded, not fixed.

- Unresolved `(ref)` placeholders with no source: four in industry
  decarbonisation, one in federal skills (the 11,065 additional workers by
  2029 figure), one in federal procurement (the ESP Policy threshold), three
  in state R&D.
- Citations with no matching entry in their section's reference list:
  "Business Wire 2025" (federal emissions), "Allens 2024" (federal

  is titled *Climate Change Bill 2023* while the sentence is about the
  *Climate Action this Decade Bill 2025*.

  2024; its own reference is dated 24 April 2025.
- Two reference URLs came through as Google redirect links and one
  (Fortescue, 2025b) as `/articles/,-w-,`. The redirects were unwrapped; the
  broken one is recorded without a link.
- Typos carried in both report and site: "Fortecue", "Forescue", "PIlbara",
